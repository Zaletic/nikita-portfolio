DAU RidePod

A self-service e-bike vending kiosk and smart docking system that lets DAU students rent a bike for short trips off campus in under a minute, designed through the lens of human factors in interaction design.

Then we asked the administration.

Student research pointed to costly short trips and no food late at night. We had not yet heard DAU’s side, so we interviewed Mr. Kirit Pandya, Assistant Administrative Officer.

What students told us

The starting point

Travel

Trips toward InfoCity or town cost a lot, and vehicles are not readily available.

Late-night food

The canteen closes at night and students cannot get food, though this sits with hostel management.

What the administration told us

Four reasons the e-bike model would not work

01

Low student demand

An earlier campus experiment found students were not keen on the service. An MoU with GMC for a service desk near the gate was shelved after a joint meeting with student representatives and the Director.

02

Ride-hailing already wins

A 3 km trip costs about ₹20–22 on a bike-taxi, ₹24 to the hospital, and a local bus starts at ₹40. Ride-hailing also picks up on campus and drops at the door.

03

Campus policy

DAU treats the campus as a home. It follows a non-polluting vehicle policy, using bicycles and electric load carriers internally, and does not want more motorised vehicles.

04

Numbers do not add up

A vendor must recover purchase and maintenance costs. Without steady student use there is no business volume, and cheap alternatives are everywhere.

“I strongly advise you to pivot to a different model rather than e-bikes.”

Mr. Kirit Pandya, Assistant Administrative Officer, DAU

Late-night food

Already being solved

The administration plans automated vending kiosks over the next one to two months, so a full canteen does not need to stay open. They will sit in open, easy-to-reach places near the boys’ hostel, the girls’ hostel, the central block and the administrative area.

Where the administration pointed us

Two directions worth developing

A

In-house digital queue management

A module for the campus cafeterias, built into the official student mobile app. It would automate orders and payments so students do not wait in long lines.

B

Campus waste management

Scientific, eco-friendly ways to reduce, segregate and dispose of waste across campus.

Whichever direction we pick, the administration asked for a model that serves the wider campus community: students, staff and visitors.

Source: interview with the DAU administration, transcript translated into English.
